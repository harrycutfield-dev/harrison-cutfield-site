Why use a buyers agent

Three things that change when you have one.

1

Everyone else is paid by the seller.

The listing agent, the stager, the photographer, the copywriter. Their job is the best price for the vendor. Mine is the best outcome for you.

2

I see homes before they are advertised.

Off market, pre launch, and the ones that quietly come back after a campaign stalls. Most of the best buying on the Shore never makes it to a portal.

3

I know what the house down the road actually sold for.

Not the portal estimate. The number, the terms, how many offers there were and why it went that way. That is the difference between a fair offer and an overpaid one.

Do you know the process?

Five steps, and how to be the buyer they say yes to.

Most buyers lose the home they wanted at the offer stage, not the search stage. Here is the sequence, and the four things that make your offer the clean one on the table.

Pre approval, not pre qualification

A real pre approval from a broker, in writing, with the conditions spelt out. It tells us how hard we can push.

Shortlist

I send what fits, you tell me what lands. We go and look, early and often, before the open homes.

Due diligence before the offer

Builders report, LIM, title, body corporate if there is one, lawyer across the contract. Done before you commit, not after.

The offer

Written on the Sale and Purchase Agreement, priced to the evidence, with as few conditions as you can safely carry.

Unconditional and settlement

Deposit paid, finance confirmed, insurance in place from the moment you go unconditional, keys on settlement day.

Strengthen your position. A clean offer wins against a bigger one more often than you would think.

Fewer conditions

Every clause is a reason for the vendor to pick someone else. Do the work up front so you can offer with a short finance clause, or none at all.

Deposit ready

Ten percent sitting in the right account on the day. Cash in hand beats a bigger number with strings attached.

Settlement that suits them

Ask what date the vendor wants. Matching it costs you nothing and it often wins the deal.

Lawyer briefed early

Your solicitor has the title and the contract before you offer, so there is no delay when the vendor says yes.
